Why Electricity Prices Keep Rising: The Grid Interconnection Crisis No One Explains

Based on the title metadata, the video links rising electricity prices to a “grid interconnection crisis,” arguing that problems connecting new generation, storage, and large loads to the network are driving cost increases. It likely frames interconnection backlogs, insufficient transmission capacity, and slow upgrade/permitting processes as translation points from technical bottlenecks into higher consumer and market prices.

Key points
  • Highlights a grid interconnection crisis as a root cause behind recent electricity price increases.
  • Focuses on interconnection queue backlogs and delays that stall new capacity and add costs.
  • Points to transmission constraints and upgrade/permitting slowness as key failure points.
  • Explains cost pass-through dynamics where delays and grid fixes increase market prices.
  • Signals broader impacts for renewables, storage deployments and large new loads.
So what
  • Treat interconnection risk as a core project risk: factor queue times and upgrade timelines into bids, contracts, and financial models.
  • Coordinate earlier and more tightly between generation developers, transmission planners, and utilities to prioritize upgrades that relieve price pressure.
  • Quantify expected cost impacts from interconnection delays when planning capacity additions for data centers, AI infrastructure, and large industrial customers.
  • Advocate for process reforms (faster studies, cost-allocation clarity, staged network upgrades) to reduce backlog‑driven price effects.
  • EPCs and utilities should build contingency margins and schedule slack into delivery programs to manage interconnection-driven delays and cost escalation.
