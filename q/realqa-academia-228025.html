“Your paper will be rejected if a reviewer can generate a better paper by using LLMs.”

The short answer

No, papers are not rejected for being outperformed by LLM-generated work.

Explore the answer

In computer science conferences, the review process follows strict rules focusing solely on the submitted paper itself. Reviewers do not compare submissions to papers generated by large language models (LLMs) or use LLM outputs as a benchmark. The purpose of peer review is to assess the quality and contribution of the submitted work, not to evaluate it against AI-generated alternatives. Additionally, many venues prohibit fully LLM-generated papers, so such submissions are generally not publishable. Therefore, the claim that a paper will be rejected if a reviewer can produce a better paper using LLMs is not aligned with standard review practices.

Grok's perspective

Rejection decisions hinge on factors like novelty, rigor, and clarity, which could indirectly intersect with LLM capabilities but lack established direct linkage in current practices. Policies may shift as tools evolve, warranting ongoing attention to guidelines.
