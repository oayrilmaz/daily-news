What type of parasite causes a woody flower-like growth in tree branches?

The short answer

Parasitic plants from the mistletoe family cause woody flower-like growths.

Explore the answer

Woody flower-like growths on tree branches, often called woodroses or burls, are caused by parasitic plants, particularly those from the mistletoe family. These parasites produce a root-like structure called a haustorium that penetrates the host tree's tissues to extract nutrients. The tree reacts by forming a woody outgrowth around this intrusion. The seeds of these parasitic plants are spread by fruit-eating birds, which deposit them on tree branches where they germinate. Such growths are common in regions like Indonesia and parts of Africa, where they are collected for ornamental use. Examples of parasitic plants involved include Pedistylis galpinii and Erianthemum dregei, which infect host trees such as Sclerocarya birrea and Chinaberry. The resulting woodrose varies in color depending on the host tree species.
