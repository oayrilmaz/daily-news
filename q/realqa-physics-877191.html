High school physics question on rotational equilibrium

The short answer

Net torque changes angular momentum, not always spin rate.

Explore the answer

A non-zero net torque on an object causes its angular momentum to change over time, as described by the equation τ = dL/dt. However, this change in angular momentum does not necessarily mean the object changes its spin rate. For example, a rod being accelerated can have its center of mass gain momentum in a direction not aligned with a chosen reference point, causing the angular momentum about that point to change. When considering angular momentum about the rod's center of mass, only rotational motion contributes, simplifying the analysis. Additionally, if a point on the rod is considered at rest, this implies a non-inertial frame where fictitious forces appear. These fictitious forces produce torques that balance the net torque, resulting in zero total torque in that frame. Thus, understanding torque and angular momentum depends on the reference point and frame of analysis.
