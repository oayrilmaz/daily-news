Could a transformer without a secondary winding exist?

The short answer

Yes, a transformer can exist without a secondary winding.

Explore the answer

A transformer typically has a primary and a secondary winding, where the secondary winding allows current to flow through a connected load, influencing the primary current via electromagnetic feedback. However, if instead of a secondary winding, a simple piece of metal is placed near the primary winding, currents are still induced in this metal. These induced currents create a magnetic field that opposes the primary magnetic field, similar to how a secondary winding's load affects the primary. This feedback reduces the electromotive force in the primary winding, allowing current to flow more easily. Therefore, the principle of electromagnetic induction and feedback can occur even without a traditional secondary winding, meaning a transformer-like effect can exist without a secondary coil.

Grok's perspective

Standard transformer definitions rely on mutual induction between windings for voltage transfer. Devices lacking a secondary may instead operate as inductors, depending on precise terminology used.
