When is an offensive lineman allowed to move?

The short answer

An offensive lineman can move smoothly before the snap without simulating it.

Explore the answer

According to college football rules, offensive linemen are allowed to move before the snap as part of a smooth, rhythmical shift or motion. However, all offensive players must come to a complete stop for at least one full second before the ball is snapped. The key restriction is that no player should make quick, jerky movements that could simulate the start of the play. Movements like shifting feet, shoulders, arms, or head in a sudden way are not allowed. The purpose of these rules is to prevent players from mimicking the snap to confuse the defense. Officials judge whether the movement is a legal shift or an attempt to simulate the snap. This principle is consistent in college football and likely similar in the NFL.
