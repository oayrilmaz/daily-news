What was the main purpose in Japan trying to capture the Dutch East Indies oil fields intact?

The short answer

To use the oil production and refining capacity immediately for war.

Explore the answer

Japan aimed to capture the Dutch East Indies oil fields intact to quickly access their oil production and refining facilities. This was crucial because Japan depended heavily on imported oil and needed to sustain its military operations during the war. Although some facilities were sabotaged, Japan managed to restore much of the production and refining capacity within a short time, allowing them to maintain fleet and air operations. However, despite restoring production, transporting the oil to Japan became increasingly difficult due to effective U.S. submarine attacks on shipping routes. This limited the amount of oil reaching Japan, causing shortages later in the war. The initial goal was to replace lost oil supplies from the U.S. and support a swift war in the Pacific, but while the capture of the oil fields succeeded, the overall war plan ultimately failed due to logistical challenges.
