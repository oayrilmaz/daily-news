What is the most extreme weather found on another planet?

The short answer

HD 189733b has the most extreme known weather with molten glass rain.

Explore the answer

The exoplanet HD 189733b exhibits some of the most extreme weather conditions known outside our Solar System. Its atmosphere reaches temperatures over 1000°C, accompanied by incredibly strong winds traveling at about 7000 kilometers per hour. Remarkably, it experiences rain made of molten glass that falls sideways due to these fierce winds. Another planet, HD 80606 b, may have even faster winds—up to 3 miles per second—caused by rapid heating during its eccentric orbit, but these winds are not continuous and it does not have molten glass rain. Therefore, HD 189733b is considered a prime example of extreme extraterrestrial weather due to the combination of its high temperatures, intense wind speeds, and unusual precipitation.

Grok's perspective

Assessing claims of extreme planetary weather depends heavily on limited observational data and how "extreme" is defined across varying atmospheric conditions. Indirect measurements often leave room for alternative interpretations of such phenomena.
