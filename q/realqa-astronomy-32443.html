Observable universe equals its Schwarzschild radius (event horizon)?

The short answer

No, the observable universe's radius is not equal to its Schwarzschild radius.

Explore the answer

The observable universe has a radius of about 46.3 billion light years, much larger than the often-cited 13.8 billion years age. Its total mass, including all matter and energy, leads to a Schwarzschild radius of approximately 475 billion light years, which is much greater than the observable radius. This means the observable universe is much smaller than its Schwarzschild radius. However, this does not imply the universe is a black hole, because a black hole is a region within space, whereas the universe encompasses all of space with no 'outside'. The confusion often arises from using the Hubble radius (about 13.8 billion light years) as the universe's radius, which is smaller and leads to coincidental equalities but no physical equivalence. The critical density of the universe relates to this radius and mass but does not mean the universe is a black hole.
