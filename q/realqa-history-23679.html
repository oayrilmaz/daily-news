Who liberated Denmark in WWII?

The short answer

Denmark was liberated mainly by British forces in May 1945.

Explore the answer

Denmark's liberation from German occupation in World War II occurred in May 1945, primarily through the efforts of British forces led by Field Marshal Bernard Montgomery. The easternmost island of Bornholm was an exception, as it was liberated by Soviet forces who stayed there for nearly a year afterward. Unlike many other regions, Denmark did not experience significant battles during its liberation; instead, the Allies occupied or took control of German garrisons after Germany's surrender. This means the liberation was more of a post-surrender occupation rather than a combat-driven liberation.
