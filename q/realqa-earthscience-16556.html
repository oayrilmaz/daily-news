Why do high tides occur simultaneously on opposite sides of the Earth?

The short answer

Because Earth's water is pulled and pushed by the Moon's gravity and inertia.

Explore the answer

High tides occur simultaneously on opposite sides of the Earth due to the combined effects of the Moon's gravitational pull and the inertia of Earth's water. The side of Earth facing the Moon experiences a bulge because the Moon's gravity pulls water toward it. On the opposite side, inertia causes water to bulge outward as it tends to continue moving in a straight line while Earth and the Moon rotate around their common center of mass. This creates two tidal bulges on opposite sides of Earth. As Earth rotates, these bulges cause high tides at different locations. Although this two-bulge model is a simplification and real tides are influenced by factors like ocean basin shapes and Earth's rotation, it explains why high tides happen roughly simultaneously on opposite sides of the planet.
