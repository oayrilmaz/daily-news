Does every spark gap create a radio wave or EM wave?

The short answer

Not every spark gap effectively creates radio or EM waves.

Explore the answer

A spark gap by itself, such as in a lighter, does produce some electromagnetic radiation, but this emission is extremely weak. In setups like Hertz's experiment, the spark gap functions mainly as a switch that allows a charged dipole to discharge and then oscillate due to its capacitance and inductance. This oscillation creates a damped alternating current that emits radio waves at a specific frequency. Therefore, the spark gap alone is not the main source of radio waves; it is the combination with a resonant system that generates significant electromagnetic radiation.

Grok's perspective

The production of electromagnetic waves from a spark gap can depend on factors like circuit design and discharge characteristics. Outcomes may differ substantially across setups.
