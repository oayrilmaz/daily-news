Why are two currency options available when paying via card at airports abroad?

The short answer

To choose who handles currency conversion and control fees.

Explore the answer

When paying by card abroad, two currency options appear to let you decide who converts the payment: either the local currency or your card's currency. Selecting the local currency means your card issuer converts the amount using their exchange rate plus possible fees, which might be more favorable if you have a good card. Choosing your card's currency means the merchant's bank does the conversion, letting you see the exact charge in your currency immediately, but often at a less favorable rate. This choice affects the exchange rate and any additional fees, so for large purchases, comparing rates is beneficial. For small purchases, knowing your card's foreign transaction fees helps decide which option is better.
