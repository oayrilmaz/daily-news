What is nothing in cosmology, and how is it different from vacuum?

The short answer

Nothing is the absence of spacetime; vacuum is empty spacetime.

Explore the answer

In cosmology, 'nothing' refers to the complete absence of spacetime itself, mathematically represented by the empty manifold (∅). This means there are no points, no distances, no time, and no events—essentially no physical existence at all. In contrast, a vacuum is a region of spacetime that is empty of matter and energy but still exists as a structure within spacetime. Different types of vacua exist, such as the quantum vacuum filled with fluctuations, the general relativistic vacuum with zero stress-energy, and classical or technical vacuums. Unlike 'nothing,' vacua are 'something' because spacetime persists. The concept of 'nothing' is not just an empty region but the absence of the manifold that defines space and time, making it fundamentally different from any vacuum state.
