Does Actinium-225 glow due to Cherenkov radiation?

The short answer

No, Actinium-225 glow is mainly from alpha-induced luminescence, not Cherenkov.

Explore the answer

The visible glow from Actinium-225 solutions is primarily caused by luminescence induced by alpha radiation rather than Cherenkov radiation. Although beta particles from Ac-225 can produce some Cherenkov light, this effect is minor and occurs mostly in the glass vial, whereas the observed glow is inside the solution. Alpha particles have a very short range in water and glass, causing luminescence that sharply ends at the solution-glass boundary. This alpha-induced luminescence in aqueous solutions is not fully understood but is known to produce a broad spectrum of light. The energy deposited by alpha decay in the Ac-225 decay chain is significantly greater than that from beta decay, making alpha luminescence the dominant source of the glow. Therefore, the characteristic glow of Ac-225 solutions is not due to Cherenkov radiation but rather to alpha radiation-induced luminescence.

Grok's perspective

The possibility of Cherenkov radiation contributing to any Actinium-225 emission cannot be fully excluded absent specific measurements of its spectrum and medium. Attribution to any single mechanism therefore stays tentative.
