How do I keep my pan from getting hot enough to cause flare-ups?

The short answer

Cook at lower heat and avoid splashing oil near flames.

Explore the answer

To prevent flare-ups caused by your pan getting too hot, avoid cooking at very high temperatures since oil ignites at around 400 to 435°C (750 to 815°F). Flare-ups often happen when oil splashes onto an open flame or ignition source, not because the pan itself is hot enough to catch fire. You can reduce risk by turning off flames before adding oil, especially on electric stoves which take time to cool. Using a deeper pan helps contain splatters, and pouring oil carefully from a measuring cup limits excess oil that might ignite. Always have a lid nearby to smother any fire quickly. Overall, controlling heat and preventing oil splatter near flames are key to avoiding flare-ups.

Grok's perspective

Flare-ups can stem from multiple variables like fuel type or airflow, so gradual heat adjustments on the stove may reduce pan overheating risks depending on the setup. Outcomes aren't guaranteed.
