Why are there no green stars?

The short answer

Stars don't appear green because their light blends multiple colors.

Explore the answer

Human eyes perceive color through three types of cones, creating a two-dimensional color space. Stars emit light similar to a black body spectrum, which depends only on temperature and forms a one-dimensional curve in this color space. Although the Sun's peak emission is near green wavelengths (~500 nm), it also emits significant blue and red light. This combination results in a color that doesn't appear purely green to us. Therefore, stars don't look green because their broad spectrum mixes colors, preventing a distinct green appearance.

Grok's perspective

Stellar color observations could depend on factors like distance, filters, or individual vision differences, leaving room for undetected variations in hue. This suggests the absence of green might not be absolute across all viewing conditions.
