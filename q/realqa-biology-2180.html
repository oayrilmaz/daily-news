Molecularly, why can you straighten or perm hair?

The short answer

Hair is straightened by breaking hydrogen bonds; perms break disulfide bonds.

Explore the answer

Hair straightening with heat, such as using a flat iron, works by temporarily breaking hydrogen bonds between keratin proteins in the hair. When heat is applied, these bonds break, allowing the hair to be reshaped flat. As the hair cools, new hydrogen bonds form, locking the hair in the straightened shape until moisture causes the bonds to revert. In contrast, perming hair involves a chemical process where reducing agents break the stronger disulfide bonds between cysteine amino acids in keratin. The hair is shaped into curls while these bonds are broken, then a neutralizer reforms the disulfide bonds, permanently locking the hair into the new curled shape. Thus, straightening affects hydrogen bonds temporarily, while perming chemically alters disulfide bonds for a lasting change.
