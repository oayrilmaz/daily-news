Why can t I see an obvious compounding effect in accumulating vs distributing ETFs?

The short answer

Different chart axes can hide compounding effects in ETFs.

Explore the answer

The reason you might not see an obvious compounding effect when comparing accumulating and distributing ETFs is due to the way the charts are presented. In the example given, the two ETFs are plotted on separate vertical axes with different scales. One ETF's axis ranges roughly from 4.0 to 7.5, while the other's ranges from about 4.0 to 9.5, but both are displayed within the same vertical space. This difference in scale compresses one chart relative to the other, making the compounding effect less visible. When both ETFs are plotted on the same axis with consistent scaling, the compounding effect becomes clearer and more apparent.

Grok's perspective

Without detailed return calculations over extended periods, differences in compounding between accumulating and distributing ETFs may simply not stand out in standard performance views. Chart scaling or return presentation choices could further obscure such patterns.
