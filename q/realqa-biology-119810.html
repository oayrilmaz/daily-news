Could this unusual structure found near the Narmada River in Madhya Pradesh, India, be a fossilized jaw of a prehistoric mammal?

The short answer

Yes, it is likely a fossilized jaw of a prehistoric mammal called Stegodon.

Explore the answer

The unusual structure found near the Narmada River in Madhya Pradesh is identified as a jaw belonging to Stegodon namadicus, an extinct member of the Proboscidea family, which includes elephants and their prehistoric relatives. Stegodon lived from about 6.5 million years ago until roughly 20,000 years ago, a period consistent with fossilization. The jaw features tooth plates with ridges characteristic of Proboscidea. Experts from the Geological Survey of India have examined the specimen and confirmed its identity as a Stegodon fossil. This discovery aligns with known prehistoric fauna of the region and supports the conclusion that the structure is indeed a fossilized jaw of a prehistoric mammal.
