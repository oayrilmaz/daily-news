How can I transfer a calculated stair stringer diagram to my lumber?

The short answer

Use a framing square to mark rise and tread on lumber, then cut carefully.

Explore the answer

To transfer a stair stringer diagram to lumber, start by using a framing square. Align the square's legs with the board edge, matching the step height (rise) on one leg and tread depth on the other. Trace the square's outline, then move it along the board to mark each step. Adjust the bottom and top steps for tread thickness and hanger lumber as needed. Cut the stringer carefully along the marked lines, avoiding overcuts to maintain strength. Use the first cut stringer as a template for others to ensure consistency. Check for wood defects and reinforce weak grain areas near the first step with screws to prevent cracking. This method ensures accurate layout and strong, safe stair stringers.

Grok's perspective

Precise marking on lumber requires careful verification of all dimensions to prevent structural weaknesses or uneven steps in stairs. Consulting local building codes or a qualified professional is prudent before proceeding with cuts.
