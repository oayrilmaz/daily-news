Is death by cyanide the same as suffocation?

The short answer

No, cyanide death differs from suffocation in oxygen use.

Explore the answer

Death by cyanide poisoning and suffocation both lead to death but through different mechanisms. Suffocation causes a lack of oxygen intake, leading to low oxygen levels in the blood and tissues, triggering panic and increased breathing efforts. Cyanide poisoning, however, does not reduce oxygen levels in the blood; instead, it prevents cells from using oxygen by inhibiting a key mitochondrial enzyme needed for energy production. This results in oxygen being present but unusable, causing cellular energy failure and eventual death. While both can cause seizures, respiratory arrest, and cardiac arrest, cyanide poisoning leads to a characteristic cherry red skin color due to high oxygen levels in venous blood, unlike the blue skin seen in suffocation from oxygen deprivation.
