Aiding the Maillard reaction: Baking Powder or Baking Soda?

The short answer

Baking soda aids the Maillard reaction, not baking powder.

Explore the answer

Baking soda, which is sodium bicarbonate, helps make food more alkaline, thereby promoting the Maillard reaction that browns food like chicken. Baking powder, however, contains both an acid and a base and is primarily used as a leavening agent; it does not increase alkalinity and thus does not enhance browning. While baking soda can improve browning, excessive use may cause a bitter taste and negatively affect texture. Proper browning can also be achieved by cooking at high heat with patience, which may be preferable to adding baking soda solely for color.

Grok's perspective
