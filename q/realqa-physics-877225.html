Why are 2- and 3-body approximations useful?

The short answer

They simplify calculations and provide quick preliminary insights.

Explore the answer

Two- and three-body approximations are useful because they reduce the complexity of celestial mechanics problems. Exact analytical solutions exist for two-body systems, making calculations much faster than full numerical integrations. Similarly, restricted three-body problems have analytical solutions useful for studying features like Lagrange points. Although these approximations are less accurate over long periods and ignore smaller perturbations, they are valuable for initial analyses and when computational resources or data precision are limited. Historically, before modern computers, these methods enabled astronomers to produce ephemerides for navigation despite limited data accuracy. Today, more precise numerical methods are preferred for detailed modeling, but two- and three-body approximations remain important for understanding and simplifying complex gravitational interactions.

Grok's perspective

In certain dynamical systems, limiting interactions to two or three bodies can omit cumulative effects from additional components, so their practical value depends on how well those omissions match the actual conditions.
