  • Supply-chain and critical-materials stress—across transformers, batteries, and power electronics—could lengthen procurement lead times and prioritize domestic sourcing or stockpiling.
  • Policy, permitting, and financing frictions are interacting with rapid infrastructure demand, creating potential chokepoints for transmission, EPC capacity, and grid-scale storage deployment.
  • Data-center and AI load growth signals combined with signals of constrained transformer and EPC capacity may produce concentrated procurement and scheduling risk at the substation level, which would increase pressure on short-term grid reliability and capital planning.
  • Rising demand for battery storage and power electronics plus indications of critical-mineral processing limits could propagate manufacturing and delivery delays, which may force deployment trade-offs (e.g., prioritize resiliency for critical loads vs. broad capacity expansion).
  • Data-center load growth → substation pressure → transformer demand → procurement lead-time risk.
  • Renewable build acceleration → grid variability → BESS demand → lithium and cathode-materials pressure → battery manufacturing bottlenecks.
