Power-electronics demand shifts supplier priorities toward silicon carbide

Rising demand for medium- and high-voltage power-electronics in grid-tied converters is increasing silicon carbide (SiC) procurement pressure, with downstream manufacturing capacity showing near-term constraints.

Why it matters
A shift in component scarcity from metals toward semiconductors could make power-electronics supply chains the primary gating factor for inverter-heavy grid upgrades, altering procurement timelines across transmission and storage projects.

SiC-based devices offer efficiency gains for converters and MV/HV inverters, driving OEMs to redesign modules. However, SiC wafer processing and packaging capacity are more concentrated than legacy silicon device lines, which could create semiconductor-related bottlenecks for grid equipment deliveries.

If semiconductor supply tightness persists, OEMs may prioritize certain converter builds — e.g., HVDC, large inverters — and delay smaller retrofit orders, shifting where upgrades occur first.
