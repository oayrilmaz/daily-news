AI-enabled predictive maintenance adoption may reduce outage durations but raise sensor demand

Signals indicate growing trials and deployments of AI-based predictive maintenance for substations; if scaled, this may lower unplanned outage durations but increase demand for high-fidelity sensors and analytics services.

Why Cosmos noticed this today

Scaling AI-based predictive maintenance may improve reliability but will increase demand for sensors, communications, and analytics labor.

What is driving it
  • AI enables more accurate prediction of failures when fed quality sensor data. 82% confidence
  • Effective predictive maintenance depends on dense and reliable sensor inputs. 80% confidence
  • Grid operators are key adopters who support pilot-to-scale transitions of predictive maintenance. 70% confidence

AI-driven analytics can improve failure prediction and maintenance scheduling, reducing downtime and targeted replacement. However, effective predictive maintenance depends on wide sensor coverage, data integration, and skilled analytics teams. Scaling this approach could create new procurement priorities for sensors and communications gear while shifting labor needs toward data and analytics roles.

There is also a risk of uneven adoption, where well-funded regions reduce outages while others continue to face legacy-driven reliability issues.

Why it matters
Widespread predictive maintenance adoption changes procurement toward sensing and software, reallocates labor to analytics, and could widen reliability differences between regions if not evenly adopted.
