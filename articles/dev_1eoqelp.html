Signals of growing interest in modular HVDC and factory-assembled substations

Procurement and design notices indicate increased experimentation with modular HVDC and factory-assembled substation modules, which could shorten field installation time but raise integration complexity.

Why Cosmos noticed this today

Modular HVDC and factory-assembled substation approaches may shorten field schedules but increase integration and commissioning requirements. Broader uptake would shift supply-side pressure and skill needs.

What is driving it
  • Manufacturing capacity supplies modular substation units; if constrained, adoption is limited in scenario framing. 74% confidence
  • Modular approaches could enable faster HVDC site installation but require HVDC-specific manufacturing coordination. 66% confidence
  • Grid operators will need to define test and integration protocols for factory-assembled modules to ensure reliability. 70% confidence

Stakeholders are evaluating pre-fabricated HVDC modules and factory-assembled substation components to reduce on-site labor exposure and compress schedules. Early testing and pilot procurements suggest these approaches could lower installation time but require closer factory–site interface work and specific commissioning expertise.

Wider adoption may reduce some schedule risk and civil works needs, but would also shift supply-chain pressure toward modular manufacturers and skilled commissioning resources. Integration with existing AC networks and protection schemes remains a central technical unknown in the near term.

Why it matters
Modular transmission solutions intersect with transformer and EPC constraints: they could relieve site labor requirements but increase dependence on specialized manufacturers and commissioning teams.
