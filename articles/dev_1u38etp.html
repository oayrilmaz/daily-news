Processing capacity limits concentrate risk in critical-minerals supply chains

While raw ore extraction is increasing in several jurisdictions, processing and refining capacity for key minerals (e.g., copper, certain battery precursors) remains a common chokepoint that could create regional material tightness.

Why it matters
Processing limits link mineral geography to equipment production risk: if concentrated, they would increase procurement risk for copper-intensive components and favor suppliers with secured feedstock streams.

Mining and extraction alone do not eliminate supply risk: metallurgy and refining capacity determines usable output for manufacturers. When processing is constrained, downstream OEMs face variable feedstock availability and longer term contracts become more valuable.

Market participants are reassessing sourcing strategies, including longer contracts, vertical integration, and material substitution where feasible. Processing constraints can feed through to transformer windings, busbars, and conductor supply.
