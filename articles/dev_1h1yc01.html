Scenario: Competing demand for copper and lithium tightens sourcing windows

Current signals indicate simultaneous demand increases for copper (transformer windings, cabling) and lithium (battery storage), which could tighten sourcing windows and increase delivery uncertainty for grid and storage projects.

Why Cosmos noticed this today

Converging copper and lithium demand across power and transport sectors could compress supplier capacity and raise procurement risk for grid and storage projects.

What is driving it
  • Critical minerals like lithium are inputs required for battery manufacturing, making mineral flows a determinant of battery supply. 80% confidence
  • Copper is a primary material required for transformer windings and other components. 83% confidence
  • Logistics disruptions can delay delivery of critical minerals to battery manufacturers, impacting output schedules. 76% confidence

Transformers and batteries both rely on copper; batteries add demand for lithium. When multiple sectors accelerate procurement at the same time—grid upgrades, EV rollouts, storage—suppliers may prioritize customers, extend lead times, or raise prices.

This creates a procurement risk where projects may be delayed or scaled back, and utilities may need to re-sequence investments or seek alternative materials and designs.

Why it matters
Tight sourcing windows for key minerals may force reprioritization of projects, slow resilience upgrades, and create second-order financial and scheduling pressures across energy and transport investments.
