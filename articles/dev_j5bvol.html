Localized transmission congestion may accelerate modular HVDC and retrofits

Indicators suggest recurring congestion at certain cross-border corridors could push system operators toward modular HVDC solutions and converter retrofits as faster mitigations compared with large-line builds.

Why Cosmos noticed this today

Transmission congestion in constrained corridors may favor modular HVDC and converter retrofits, redirecting component demand to power-electronics and affecting supply-chain risk concentration.

What is driving it
  • HVDC implementations rely on converter transformers as core components for converter stations. 70% confidence
  • Congestion or lack of transmission capacity can increase renewable curtailment and shape integration strategies. 75% confidence
  • Converter-based retrofits are used to manage renewable flows and integrate variable generation into constrained corridors. 65% confidence

Where reinforcement via new AC lines is delayed by permitting or land constraints, modular HVDC and converter-based upgrades are an emerging mitigation signal. These options shift procurement demand from bulk conductor and tower suppliers toward power-electronics and converter transformers.

If adopted at scale, the procurement profile moves to fewer, more specialized components with different manufacturing bottlenecks and critical-material exposure, altering both schedules and risk concentrations for transmission projects.

Why it matters
Shifts from large AC line projects to modular HVDC/converter retrofits connect transmission planning decisions with demand for specialized equipment and materials, impacting procurement risk and timelines across projects.
