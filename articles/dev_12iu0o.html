Renewables curtailment trends may accelerate without targeted transmission flexibility

Current operational signals point to rising curtailment in areas with rapid wind and solar additions where transmission flexibility and storage deployment lag behind generation growth.

Why it matters
Higher curtailment reduces emissions benefits and financial returns for renewables while increasing pressure for transmission and storage investments.

Where new variable generation outpaces transmission upgrades and flexible balancing resources, curtailment can become a near‑term mechanism to maintain system balance. This results in lost renewable energy and reduced project revenues.

Targeted investments in flexibility—fast‑dispatch storage, dynamic line ratings, demand response—can reduce curtailment. Policymakers and system planners need to prioritize interconnection capacity and flexibility procurement in high‑growth zones.
