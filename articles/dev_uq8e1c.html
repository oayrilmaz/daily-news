Scenario: Aging substations and quicker replacements create procurement competition

Signals from asset-health indicators and regional investment programs could point to a scenario where aging substation fleets require faster-than-expected transformer and switchgear replacements, compressing procurement windows.

Why Cosmos noticed this today

Accelerated replacements for aging substations may generate regional procurement competition, increasing costs and schedule risk.

What is driving it
  • Aging substations often require transformer replacements to maintain capacity and reliability. 76% confidence
  • Substation replacements typically involve coordinated switchgear upgrades to ensure protection and compatibility. 72% confidence
  • How procurement is organized will influence lead times and access to available transformer inventory. 70% confidence

If asset-health trends are worse than scheduled replacements assumed, utilities may accelerate replacement of transformers and protection equipment. Simultaneous acceleration across multiple utilities within a region could create short-term competition for both equipment and installation crews.

This scenario would increase reliance on expedited logistics, temporary generation or load-management measures, and reallocation of capital from other projects.

Why it matters
Simultaneous replacement needs across distribution networks could shift resource allocation away from new-build projects and raise operational exposure during transition periods.
