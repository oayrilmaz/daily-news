Battery recycling signals could moderate cobalt demand but informal-mining dynamics remain a systemic risk

Scenario indicators point to scaling battery-recycling pilots that may reduce primary cobalt demand over a multi-year horizon, but persistent informal-mining activity and structural market dynamics could limit near-term relief.

Why it matters
Shifts in recycled-material availability alter upstream sourcing strategies for battery and transformer manufacturing and influence long-term project economics.

Recycling initiatives and circular-economy pilots are showing early throughput increases in certain regions; recovered materials can feed battery manufacturing chains if collection and refining scale.

However, informal and artisanal mining activity remains a material factor in cobalt supply dynamics, creating volatility and reputational risk for downstream firms. Policy incentives that accelerate formal recycling and refinery capacity could shift supply composition but require coordinated investment and standardization.

Market participants should watch recycled-material quality, refining capacity buildouts, and enforcement of mining supply-chain traceability as early signals of a substantive supply shift.
