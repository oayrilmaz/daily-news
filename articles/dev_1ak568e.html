Refining and upstream flows amplify critical-mineral procurement uncertainty

Signals suggest that concentrated sourcing and limited refining capacity could create bottlenecks for minerals used across transformers, batteries, and other grid equipment.

Why Cosmos noticed this today

Concentrated upstream material flows and limited refining capacity may increase procurement risk for multiple grid equipment classes. Diversification strategies carry their own technical and regulatory trade-offs.

What is driving it
  • Raw minerals need refining capacity to be converted into forms usable by manufacturers of batteries and transformers. 80% confidence
  • Refining bottlenecks influence downstream schedules and supplier availability across supply chains. 75% confidence
  • Multiple manufacturing sectors draw on the same refined-material pools, increasing scheduling competition. 66% confidence

Upstream concentration of ore and limited downstream refining can create single points of schedule risk that affect multiple equipment streams simultaneously.

Competition among industries for refined material increases the chance that projects face either higher cost or slower delivery windows.

Diversifying processing options or accepting alternative chemistries could mitigate risk but require technical validation and potential regulatory steps.

Why it matters
