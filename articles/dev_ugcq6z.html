EPC backlogs may extend substation schedules and increase interim reliability measures

EPC capacity constraints and skilled workforce shortages could extend substation project schedules, leading operators to adopt interim measures such as temporary substations, load transfers, or rental transformers.

Why Cosmos noticed this today

EPC bottlenecks can extend project timelines and force reliance on temporary measures, raising costs and potentially delaying broader grid modernization.

What is driving it
  • Multiple projects vie for limited skilled labor, affecting schedule adherence. 72% confidence
  • EPC capacity and scheduling determine substation completion timing. 78% confidence
  • Rental or temporary equipment can provide short-term service while permanent work is delayed. 68% confidence

When EPC availability is limited, projects compete for the same skilled personnel and field resources, lengthening schedules. Interim mitigation strategies (temporary assets or rentals) provide stopgaps but can be costlier and less efficient.

If backlogs persist, project planners may prioritize sites based on operational risk, leaving lower-priority upgrades deferred and potentially increasing localized reliability risk over the medium term.

Why it matters
Prolonged EPC backlogs will propagate through project portfolios, increasing near-term costs for utilities and potentially delaying critical reinforcement needed to support load growth.
