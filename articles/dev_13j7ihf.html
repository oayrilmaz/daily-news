Second-life batteries for grid services signal pilot scaling but standards gaps remain

Indications from pilot programs suggest increasing use of second-life EV batteries for frequency response and peak shaving, but standards and end-of-life frameworks may lag, creating uncertainty for scaling.

Why it matters
Standards gaps create commercial and safety uncertainty, affecting investor confidence and the pace of circular battery economy development.

Second-life battery pilots have shown potential for low-cost frequency response and localized services. However, standardization for testing, state-of-health reporting, and safety certification is still maturing.

Without clear standards, deployment may face insurance and performance-risk hurdles. Scaling second-life solutions will likely depend on harmonized testing regimes and clear recycling or warranty pathways.
