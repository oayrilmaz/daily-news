Distribution two-way flows increasing protection upgrade needs

Signals of rising distributed energy resource (DER) penetration are creating more frequent reverse flows on distribution feeders, prompting protection and relay coordination challenges at substations.

Why Cosmos noticed this today

Higher DER penetration is likely to increase demand for protection system upgrades and engineering services at substations.

What is driving it
  • Bidirectional flows from DERs increase the complexity and need for updated protection relay settings and hardware. 78% confidence
  • Relay configuration determines a substation's fault-detection and isolation capabilities under new flow patterns. 76% confidence
  • System integrators provide the installation and commissioning services required for updated protection solutions. 72% confidence

Two-way power flows complicate existing protection schemes and can raise coordination risk for fault isolation. Utilities may need to deploy adaptive protection, updated relay settings, or sectionalizing upgrades.

These adaptations require qualified engineering resources and may increase demand for digital protection relays and consulting services.

Why it matters
Protection upgrade demand interacts with OEM and EPC capacity limits; without timely upgrades, DER expansion could increase operational risk and curtailment.
