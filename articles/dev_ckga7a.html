Scenario: Substation automation and AI controls increase cyber risk surface

Signals show accelerated adoption of automation and AI-assisted control in substations; this could expand operational efficiency but also enlarge the cyber-attack surface and operational risk if cybersecurity lags.

Why Cosmos noticed this today

Faster substation automation adoption may raise cybersecurity exposure. Investment in security and operational procedures will be critical to realize benefits safely.

What is driving it
  • AI capabilities can enable adaptive protection and predictive maintenance functions in substation automation platforms. 77% confidence
  • Automation changes how operators monitor and intervene in grid operations, requiring new procedures and training. 74% confidence
  • Stronger cybersecurity practices reduce the operational and intrusion risk associated with automated assets. 72% confidence

Automation and AI tools promise faster fault detection and adaptive protection, but integrating these systems with legacy SCADA and physical assets introduces complexity. If cybersecurity measures are not scaled alongside automation, operators could face higher exposure to intrusion or misconfiguration.

Utilities may respond by segmenting networks, increasing investment in security-hardening, or delaying AI features — each with cost and timeline implications for modernization programs.

Why it matters
The push for automation connects operational efficiency goals with cyber risk management; gaps in either domain could lead to operational disruptions and slower modernization.
