Transmission bottlenecks signal localized renewable curtailment risk

Signals indicate pockets of transmission congestion that may be increasing curtailment risk for nearby wind and solar resources if bi-directional mitigation is not implemented.

Why Cosmos noticed this today

Transmission constraints could produce localized curtailment and market impacts for renewables, with implications for dispatch, revenues, and the need for grid investments.

What is driving it
  • Transmission constraints can reduce the deliverable output of renewables by forcing curtailment in constrained scenarios. 68% confidence
  • Condition of transmission infrastructure and congestion directly affects the probability and severity of curtailment events. 70% confidence
  • Curtailment changes dispatch and can alter local pricing and settlement outcomes in energy markets. 60% confidence

Transmission congestion reduces the ability to move variable generation to load centers. In constrained corridors, renewable output may be curtailed to maintain system balance.

If congestion signals intensify, market signals and operational interventions (e.g., redispatch) would likely increase, affecting revenue for generators and changing short-term dispatch patterns.

Why it matters
Curtailment driven by transmission constraints links renewable deployment outcomes to transmission investment and operational decisions; if sustained, this could shift where storage and grid upgrades are prioritized.
