Capacity market signals may favor storage but interconnection queues could blunt near-term project viability

Signals from capacity procurement cycles suggest increased value for dispatchable storage capacity, but interconnection queue backlogs and conditional connection requirements may limit the number of projects that can qualify in the short term.

Why it matters
Mismatch between market incentives and physical interconnection readiness can create stranded project risk and influence investor appetite.

Capacity markets that price firm, dispatchable capacity can improve the business case for storage projects. However, developers still face interconnection uncertainties that affect project deliverability and accreditation for capacity obligations.

Projects with conditional or delayed interconnection may miss capacity accreditation windows or require staged accreditation approaches. Investors and developers may need to calibrate financing and contracting timelines to account for queue risk.

Watch for capacity market qualification lists and shifts in interconnection queue policies that could either enable or constrain the next tranche of storage projects.
