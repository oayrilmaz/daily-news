Scenario: Data-center operators move toward modular substations and BESS for resilience

Indications point to a growing preference among some data-center operators for on-site modular substations paired with battery energy storage to accelerate resilience and reduce reliance on long-lead transformer procurements.

Why Cosmos noticed this today

Modular substations combined with battery storage present a short-to-midterm resilience option for data centers facing transformer procurement or interconnection delays. Adoption depends on integration approvals and local manufacturing availability.

What is driving it
  • To achieve resilience under transformer lead-time constraints, data centers may deploy modular substations for faster commissioning. 77% confidence
  • Modular substations are frequently paired with BESS to provide backup and smoothing functions. 74% confidence
  • On-site BESS can provide market services such as frequency response and peak shaving, affecting local market dynamics. 72% confidence

Modular substations and BESS can be deployed more quickly than large civil-built substations and may reduce short-term exposure to transformer lead-time risk.

These configurations provide fast backup capacity, frequency support, and the ability to smooth demand spikes, but they require integration expertise, grid-code acceptance, and careful protection coordination with the distribution network.

If adoption accelerates, local manufacturing demand for containerized substation modules and medium-capacity BESS could rise, creating a different type of supply pressure.

Why it matters
When data-center load growth interacts with transformer lead times and project delays, modular and storage-based resilience provides a pragmatic alternative—but scaling those alternatives shifts pressure to different supply chains and regulatory acceptance processes.
