Permitting backlog could slow key high-voltage line buildouts in northern Europe

Signals from recent consultations and regional planning meetings suggest permitting and local objection timelines may extend schedules for several planned high-voltage corridors, potentially delaying renewables evacuation capacity.

Why it matters
Transmission siting delays can bottleneck renewables growth and shift costs to balancing mechanisms or curtailment.

Where proposed corridors intersect populated or environmentally sensitive areas, public consultations and legal challenges could add weeks to months to projects. That delay would compress windows for integration of wind and solar resources that depend on those lines to reach load centers.

Delays may increase reliance on constrained local balancing resources such as storage or curtailment, raising short-term system costs and complicating merchant investment cases for new generation.
