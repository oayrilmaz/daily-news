Substation-adjacent storage deployments emerge as near-term mitigation

Operators are signaling preference for deploying modular storage at or near substations as a near-term mitigation for load and variability while longer-lead hardware (transformers, switchgear) is procured.

Why Cosmos noticed this today

Modular storage placed near substations is being used as a short-term mitigation for capacity and variability risk, but this strategy links projects to battery supply dynamics.

What is driving it
  • Modular storage can provide temporary capacity and grid services at substations, reducing immediate stress while long-lead items are delivered. 76% confidence
  • Cell and module availability will determine how quickly modular storage can be deployed as mitigation. 77% confidence
  • Operator preference for short-term reliability solutions can drive storage procurement over other capital projects. 72% confidence

Modular battery systems can be deployed faster than large transformers and provide both capacity and grid services (voltage support, peak shaving). Where permits and siting allow, they serve as an expedient option to reduce immediate loading risk and defer heavy equipment procurement.

However, increased near-term storage demand will raise pressure on battery supply chains and may create competition for cells with other sectors, linking storage deployment choices back to critical-mineral and manufacturing signals.

Why it matters
Storage-as-mitigation can reduce immediate reliability exposure but shifts procurement pressure to battery supply chains; this trade-off affects project sequencing and capital allocation.
