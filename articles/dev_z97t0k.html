Scenario: Capacity-market design shifts could favor flexible resources and change procurement demand

Signals suggest policymakers and regulators are exploring market designs that value quick-response flexibility over pure capacity; if implemented, this reweights demand toward batteries, flexible gas, and fast-start resources.

Why Cosmos noticed this today

Market design that prioritizes flexibility may redirect procurement from large fixed assets to fast-response technologies, with implications for manufacturers and grid operations.

What is driving it
  • Policy makers shape the rules and valuation methods that determine how capacity markets compensate resources. 82% confidence
  • If markets value flexibility, battery storage could capture more capacity-style revenue streams. 74% confidence
  • Grid operators use dispatchable gas to provide on-demand flexibility where storage is insufficient. 70% confidence

A market preference for flexibility would change long-term procurement signals for OEMs and EPCs. Manufacturers focused on large, slow-to-deploy assets may face lower near-term demand while suppliers of fast-build storage and power-electronics could see increased orders.

Such a shift would affect grid operators' reserve strategies and could reduce the need for some large capital reinforcements, but would also increase dependence on fuel and storage chains and require updated operational protocols and market-clearing rules.

Why it matters
Shifts in market design integrate policy, market economics, and technology procurement; they could rapidly change supplier pipelines and the operational mix grid operators must manage.
