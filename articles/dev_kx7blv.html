Rooftop solar plus VPP activity could increase distribution-level congestion

Signals show heightened enrollment in virtual power plants (VPPs) that aggregate rooftop solar and batteries in Australia; this may raise distribution congestion during export peaks if not coordinated with network constraints.

Why it matters
Distribution congestion can force curtailment of distributed generation, undercutting consumer benefits and slowing VPP momentum.

Increased centralized orchestration via VPP platforms can improve aggregate value, but aggregated exports can exceed local feeder thermal limits at certain times, prompting local network interventions.

Potential mitigations include targeted export limits, feeder-level storage dispatch coordination, and revised tariff signals to shift exports or charge times.

Regulators and network operators will need clearer data-sharing arrangements with VPP aggregators to manage feeder constraints while preserving consumer value.
