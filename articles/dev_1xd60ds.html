Interconnection delays increasing renewable curtailment risk in congested regions

Signals of growing interconnection backlogs and permitting delays in regions with high renewable additions suggest curtailment risk could rise, affecting project economics and grid balancing needs.

Why Cosmos noticed this today

Interconnection and permitting delays may increase renewable curtailment and shift the economics of siting and transmission investment. Monitoring queue movement and permitting outcomes is key.

What is driving it
  • Transmission or congestion constraints directly raise the likelihood of renewable curtailment when generation exceeds local demand or export capacity. 78% confidence
  • Higher curtailment can increase near-term demand for storage as a mitigation mechanism to capture otherwise curtailed energy. 70% confidence
  • Rapid additions of renewables concentrated in certain areas alter flow patterns and can worsen congestion without accompanying transmission expansion. 74% confidence

Congested transmission corridors and slow permitting create situations where new renewable output cannot reach demand centers, producing curtailment pressure and revenue uncertainty for developers.

If backlogs persist, project timelines, investment returns, and decisions about where to site future capacity may shift, increasing the value of transmission and long-distance solutions but also heightening near-term grid reliability challenges.

Why it matters
Curtailment pressures interact with storage and HVDC planning: if curtailment rises, it increases demand for storage and long-distance transfer solutions, placing further stress on supply chains and permitting frameworks.
