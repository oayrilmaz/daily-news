Predictive maintenance adoption could reduce unplanned outages but raises data and cybersecurity considerations

The increasing use of AI-based predictive maintenance tools may signal a reduction in unplanned equipment failures, but also highlights a growing need for secure, high-quality operational data and cybersecurity controls.

Why it matters
Reduced unplanned outages improve reliability and cost outcomes, but gaps in data governance or security could negate benefits.

AI-driven anomaly detection and predictive algorithms can prioritize inspections and maintenance, potentially reducing outage rates and optimizing spare-part inventories.

However, these tools require consistent telemetry and robust data governance; they also expand attack surfaces if models and control interfaces are not secured.

Organizations deploying predictive maintenance must therefore balance operational gains with data quality programs and cybersecurity investments.
