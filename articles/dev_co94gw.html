OT–IT integration increases attack surface at substations; signal of rising interest in segmented communications

Operators are signaling increased investment in network segmentation and zero-trust approaches for substation OT as more control functions and monitoring migrate to IP-based networks.

Why Cosmos noticed this today

Greater OT–IT convergence broadens the cyber attack surface; segmentation and zero-trust approaches are emerging countermeasures that require cross-vendor coordination.

What is driving it
  • Greater OT–IT convergence increases the need for network segmentation to manage cyber risk. 80% confidence
  • Effective segmentation relies on telecom services that can support isolated traffic flows and SLAs. 74% confidence
  • Convergence affects substations by expanding the digital attack surface and changing risk profiles. 79% confidence

As substations adopt IP telemetry and remote-control capabilities, the operational technology footprint expands and creates additional attack vectors. Early adopter utilities are piloting segmentation and stricter identity models to isolate protection and control traffic from general telemetry and corporate networks.

These measures could limit damage from a single compromised node but require coordination across vendors, firmware versions, and telecom providers.

Why it matters
If segmentation and zero-trust adoption lags, increased OT–IT convergence could raise the probability that cyber incidents impact protection and control systems, potentially leading to larger operational disruptions.
