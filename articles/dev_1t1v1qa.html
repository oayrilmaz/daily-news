Scaling battery recycling could reduce primary mineral pressure for battery supply chains

Signals of increased interest in battery recycling capacity suggest potential to substitute secondary materials for some upstream critical-mineral needs, easing pressure on raw-material supply chains over time.

Why it matters
Battery recycling connects EV and storage lifecycle flows to critical-mineral supply, potentially altering material competition dynamics for power-equipment manufacturing if scaled successfully.

Investment and pilot activity in recycling facilities can, over multi-year timelines, return significant quantities of processed materials into battery and electronics supply chains. That can reduce dependency on new mining and ease some competition for materials with cable and transformer manufacturers.

However, recycling scale-up timelines and material recovery rates are uncertain; short-term tensions may persist even as medium-term substitution potential increases.
