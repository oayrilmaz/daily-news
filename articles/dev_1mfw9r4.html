HVDC converter-station equipment lead times may be delaying interconnector commissioning

Scenario signals point to specialized converter-station components and power-electronics lead times creating potential schedule slippage for HVDC interconnectors, with knock-on effects for cross-border capacity and renewable integration.

Why Cosmos noticed this today

Specialized converter-station equipment lead times could delay HVDC project schedules and reduce cross-border transfer capacity, increasing localized grid balancing risk. This scenario warrants watching supplier capacity and testing resources.

What is driving it
  • HVDC projects require converter stations to enable AC/DC conversion at endpoints. 90% confidence
  • Converter stations depend on specialized high-voltage power-electronics and components. 86% confidence
  • Availability of high-voltage equipment is constrained by specialist manufacturing and test facilities. 78% confidence

Converter stations use bespoke high-voltage power electronics and control systems that often have long procurement cycles. Multiple projects in some regions may be competing for the same limited specialist manufacturing and testing capacity.

If delays persist, interconnector commissioning schedules could slip, reducing expected transfer capability at key moments and increasing local balancing requirements. Monitoring component order backlogs and test-facility availability will help assess persistence of this pressure.

Why it matters
Delays in HVDC commissioning reduce cross-border flexibility and can amplify local curtailment and balancing needs, linking equipment manufacturing bottlenecks with operational grid stress.
