Solid-state power-electronics adoption signals alongside legacy transformer constraints

Industry signals show increasing pilots of solid-state power-electronics at substations as an alternative to large transformer upgrades; pilots focus on compact conversion and faster deployment but raise test and standards questions.

Why Cosmos noticed this today

Solid-state power-electronics pilot activity could provide alternative pathways to manage capacity constraints but requires standards and proven reliability to materially displace large-transformer procurement.

What is driving it
  • Solid-state devices may substitute for traditional transformers in some applications, reducing demand for large electromagnetic units if proven reliable. 60% confidence
  • Standards and grid-code updates are required to allow safe, interoperable deployment of solid-state technology at scale. 73% confidence
  • OEMs develop and produce the semiconductor and converter components central to solid-state solutions. 76% confidence

Solid-state transformer and converter pilots offer potential to reduce footprint and speed deployment in constrained corridors. Early deployments are testing thermal behavior, protection integration, and lifecycle reliability. Adoption would shift procurement away from some traditional transformer demand but requires standards and long-duration testing to scale.

These are early adoption signals and not widespread replacements; watch interoperability test reports and vendor qualification notices for clearer direction.

Why it matters
If solid-state equipment scales, it would reconfigure supplier competition and material demand (less copper in bulky windings, more power-electronics components), but the transition introduces interoperability and standards coordination needs.
