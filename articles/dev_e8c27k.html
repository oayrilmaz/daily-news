AI-based predictive maintenance may reduce outages but increase dependence on software platforms and cyber risk

Adoption signals for AI-driven predictive maintenance tools at substations show potential to lower forced outage rates, but they also increase dependencies on software platforms and introduce a cyber-resilience dimension that operators must address.

Why Cosmos noticed this today

AI-enabled maintenance could improve reliability yet adds software and cybersecurity dependencies that must be managed through SLAs and hardened architectures.

What is driving it
  • AI methods support analytics that anticipate equipment degradation before failures occur. 80% confidence
  • Effective predictive maintenance can reduce forced outages and improve reliability metrics. 76% confidence
  • Analytics platforms host AI models and process telemetry for predictive maintenance. 77% confidence

Predictive analytics can flag incipient equipment issues before failure, reducing emergency replacements and smoothing procurement cycles. However, reliance on cloud-based analytics and connected control platforms increases attack surface and third-party dependency.

Operators may need parallel investment in cybersecurity, vendor SLAs, and offline fallback procedures to realize benefits without increasing risk.

Why it matters
Combining physical asset benefits with new digital dependencies changes risk profiles: reliability gains may be offset by software-supply vulnerabilities and vendor concentration if not mitigated.
