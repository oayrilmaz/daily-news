Curtailment signals in high-renewable regions highlight need for flexibility and market reforms

Indicators from high-renewable regions point to rising curtailment events that could be mitigated by increased grid flexibility and market mechanisms rewarding dispatchable resources.

Why it matters
Without viable revenue for flexibility, investment in storage and demand response may lag, increasing system costs and limiting renewable integration.

Scenario: rapid solar and wind deployments in constrained areas are increasing negative-price hours and curtailment. Without additional flexibility—storage, demand response, or revised market signals—renewable projects may see reduced revenue and higher effective LCOE.

Policymakers and grid operators may need to accelerate reforms that create sustained revenue streams for flexibility providers and update market products to value fast-response services.
