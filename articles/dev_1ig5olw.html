Renewables variability increases transmission-side balancing need

High instantaneous renewable output variability in interconnected areas is producing more frequent intra-day ramping requirements, adding stress to transmission assets and nearby substations during steep net-load changes.

Why Cosmos noticed this today

Renewables-driven variability can change operational loading patterns on transmission and substation assets, prompting calls for dynamic ratings and targeted asset upgrades.

What is driving it
  • Variable output from renewables raises intra-day balancing and ramping requirements. 78% confidence
  • Battery storage is a near-term resource to provide the ramping and balancing services required by renewables variability. 74% confidence
  • High variability can cause reverse flows and atypical loading on transmission assets. 70% confidence

Periods of rapid change in renewable output create reverse flows and atypical thermal cycling in lines and transformers not sized for such patterns. Grid balancing resources (storage, flexible generation) are available but may be localized, shifting stress to certain transmission corridors.

This dynamic increases the importance of dynamic rating, storage deployment, and targeted reinforcement in constrained corridors.

Why it matters
Renewables variability ties into transformer and line stress while intersecting with limits in procurement and lead times for reinforcement projects, creating operational risk if upgrades are delayed.
