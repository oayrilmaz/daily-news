EV charging corridors are signalling local distribution peak challenges in suburban circuits

Evidence from load pattern analyses and site planning discussions suggests suburban distribution circuits near new fast-charging corridors could face increased peak loads unless coordinated demand management and feeder upgrades are implemented.

Why it matters
Unaddressed distribution stress raises reliability and safety risks and can delay deployment of charging infrastructure needed for transport electrification.

Fast-charging clusters create abrupt demand spikes that can overload secondary transformers and cause voltage excursions on long, lightly loaded feeders.

Demand-response programs and managed charging can reduce the worst peaks, but implementation requires utility tariffs, communications infrastructure, and customer-enrollment incentives. Solar PV paired with managed charging offers daytime offset opportunities but may not align with evening charging behavior.

Utilities and planners should evaluate targeted feeder reinforcement, managed-charging pilots, and non-wires alternatives for hotspot mitigation.
