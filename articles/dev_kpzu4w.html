Transmission bottlenecks causing increased curtailment risk for renewable projects

Early indications of constrained transmission capacity in several regions are leading to higher curtailment risk for new renewable projects, which may reduce near-term capacity utilization and shift investment priorities toward storage and local reinforcement.

Why Cosmos noticed this today

Transmission constraints are increasing curtailment risk, which could redirect investment to storage and reinforcement projects and create new procurement competition.

What is driving it
  • Transmission capacity limitations can reduce the ability of renewables to export energy, increasing curtailment risk. 78% confidence
  • Higher curtailment risk can increase demand for storage to firm generation and reduce energy spillage. 75% confidence
  • Storage deployment and integration require EPC capacity, which may compete with other reinforcement projects. 72% confidence

Renewable projects sited ahead of transmission upgrades face increased curtailment probability. This changes project economics and increases demand signals for storage co-location and for substation and line reinforcement projects.

If curtailment risk becomes persistent, developers and grid planners could accelerate storage tenders or prioritize reinforcements, creating competing demand for limited EPC and equipment resources.

Why it matters
Curtailment links renewable deployment to storage and substation retrofit demand, with cascading effects on EPC workloads and equipment availability.
