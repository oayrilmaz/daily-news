Scenario: Renewable curtailment risk from constrained transmission corridors

Renewable plant additions in regions with limited transmission expansion could increase curtailment signals, especially during low-demand/high-generation periods, potentially degrading project revenues and storage economics.

Why Cosmos noticed this today

If renewables additions outpace transmission capacity, curtailment rates may climb, creating stress on project economics and increasing demand for storage and grid flexibility investments.

What is driving it
  • Higher local renewable output can increase stress on constrained transmission corridors if reinforcements lag. 76% confidence
  • Congestion can force curtailment or redispatch that reduces actual energy delivered from renewables. 74% confidence
  • Storage, demand response, and operational measures can alleviate congestion and limit curtailment. 72% confidence

Grid integration of variable renewables depends on transmission capacity and flexibility. Where transmission build-out lags generation additions, dispatch order may shift toward curtailment or redispatch.

Curtailment pressures may alter the business case for new projects and raise demand for grid-flexibility solutions such as storage or dynamic line rating. Policymakers and system planners could face increased pressure to accelerate transmission upgrades or revise connection models.

Why it matters
This signal ties renewable deployment planning to transmission and storage markets; unmanaged curtailment can feed back to investor decisions and grid resilience strategies.
