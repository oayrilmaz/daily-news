AI may enable novel attack vectors on grid control systems if defensive data practices lag

Scenario indicators suggest growing AI capabilities could be repurposed to scale reconnaissance and evasion tactics against SCADA and relay systems, particularly where telemetry and access controls are weak.

Why Cosmos noticed this today

AI-driven offensive techniques could increase cyber risk to grid control systems, especially where data governance and segmentation are inadequate.

What is driving it
  • AI can be used to analyze SCADA telemetry at scale and identify vulnerabilities or anomalous patterns that could be exploited. 70% confidence
  • Compromise or disruption of SCADA systems can degrade grid resilience by impairing monitoring and control. 83% confidence
  • Protection relays play a role in isolating faults and protecting stability, contributing to resilience. 78% confidence

AI tools can automate analysis of telemetry and identify weak authentication or misconfigurations at scale. If utilities delay tightening telemetry hygiene and access segmentation, the attack surface for sophisticated actors could broaden.

Adoption of AI-driven monitoring by defenders can reduce this risk, but the balance between attacker and defender AI adoption will shape near-term risk trajectories.

Why it matters
Increased data and AI in operations improves efficiency but simultaneously raises cyber-dependency; insufficient defensive upgrades could convert efficiency gains into greater systemic vulnerability.
