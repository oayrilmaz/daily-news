Silicon-carbide demand profile tightening from EVs and inverter growth

Signals from procurement and semiconductor fabs show an uptick in ordered capacity for SiC wafers tied to EV traction inverters and grid-scale inverter demand, which could tighten availability for utility-scale projects.

Why Cosmos noticed this today

SiC demand from mobility and grid sectors is creating competition for limited fabrication capacity, with potential lead-time impacts for utilities.

What is driving it
  • Foundries and fabs produce SiC wafers and devices; fab capacity limits constrain SiC availability. 79% confidence
  • SiC improves device efficiency and thermal performance, making it desirable for advanced power-electronics. 78% confidence
  • Growing EV charging and traction markets raise demand for power-electronics which compete for SiC capacity. 75% confidence

SiC devices are increasingly specified for both EV inverters and high-efficiency renewable inverters due to performance advantages.

Fab capacity and raw-material inputs for SiC are limited relative to growing demand; prioritization by EV OEMs could divert capacity away from grid applications, lengthening utility procurement timelines.

Why it matters
Competition for SiC capacity links mobility and grid-electrification trends; prioritization decisions in one sector could materially affect deployment timelines in the other.
