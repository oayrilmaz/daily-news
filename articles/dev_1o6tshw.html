Rooftop PV signals rising reverse power flow incidents on distribution feeders

Widespread rooftop photovoltaic adoption is signaling more frequent reverse power flow on low-voltage feeders in some Australian networks, creating concerns about protection coordination and voltage management.

Why it matters
Unchecked reverse flow can impair protection, complicate voltage control, and increase short-circuit current exposure for some equipment.

High distributed PV penetration can result in midday exported energy pushing distribution voltage above acceptable limits and causing reverse power flows into upstream transformers. Protection schemes designed for unidirectional flow may not detect faults consistently under bidirectional conditions.

Network operators may need to accelerate inverter-based control upgrades, update voltage regulation equipment, or implement active network management to maintain service quality and fault detection sensitivity.
