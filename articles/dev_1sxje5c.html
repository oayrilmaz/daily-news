Scenario: On-site battery systems and microgrids increase demand for power electronics and procurement complexity

More data centers are exploring on-site batteries and microgrid controls to manage resilience and peak demand. This trend may increase near-term procurement for high-capacity inverters, power electronics, and control systems, interacting with the wider supply chain pressures for renewables.

Why Cosmos noticed this today

Data-center resilience strategies using on-site batteries and microgrids could increase demand for power electronics, adding competition to utility and renewable procurement pipelines.

What is driving it
  • Desire for resilience and peak management drives data centers to adopt on-site storage solutions in scenario framing. 76% confidence
  • Battery systems need inverters and converters to interface with the grid and loads. 82% confidence
  • Longer lead times constrain the ability of buyers to deploy on-site systems quickly. 72% confidence

On-site battery systems require grid-interfacing inverters, protection relays, and sophisticated microgrid control software. As data centers pursue resilience, they may order grid-forming inverters and energy-management systems that compete with utility-scale orders.

This competition can exacerbate allocation challenges for power electronics manufacturers and create prioritization conflicts between industrial and utility customers.

Why it matters
If data centers and utilities compete for similar inverter and control hardware, allocation decisions by manufacturers could re-prioritize deliveries, affecting both resilience and grid expansion projects.
